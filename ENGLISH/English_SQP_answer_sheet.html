🔹 SECTION A: READING SKILLS (22 marks)

📄 Passage 1: Smartphones & Connectivity

I. According to paragraph 1, what effect do smartphone notifications have on the people in the room?
Notifications constantly interrupt conversations, making people instinctively reach for their phones. The 'ping' and vibration distract them from real-life interactions, blurring the boundary between virtual and real.
II. How does the author use the metaphor of 'autumn leaves in the wind' to interpret the passing of time?
Just as autumn leaves drift away aimlessly and are lost, moments slip away unnoticed while people are engrossed in their smartphones. It suggests time passes fleetingly and irretrievably when we are distracted.
III. Complete the sentence: Choose the correct response.
A. a deep (psychological) dependence on technology for a sense of wholeness or connection. The analogy points towards psychological dependence, not physical damage.
IV. Complete suitably with ONE advantage (para 2): Designating specific hours to avoid digital devices can help...
...reduce anxiety and the 'fear of missing out', allowing individuals to be more present and mentally peaceful.
V. Why does the writer refer to digital noise as 'dissonance'? Choose:
C. It interferes with our ability to engage with meaningful aspects of our life. (Dissonance = harsh combination of sounds, here it disrupts life's true rhythm.)
VI. Provide ONE textual evidence that perpetual engagement manifests in the body (para 3).
"The weight of constant connectivity forms a hunch upon our shoulders" – showing physical strain (posture deterioration) due to continuous device use.
VII. What does 'true rhythm of life' refer to? (para 3-4)
It refers to authentic human experiences, deep connections, and meaningful moments that are lost in the chaos of digital distractions.
VIII. Complete analogy: whisper : quietly :: loudly : ? (one word from para 4)
clamour (whisper is to quietly as clamour is to loudly – antonym context).
IX. Why is the statement about 'increasingly isolated in a sea of digital faces' ironic?
A. Despite the intent to connect more effectively, the result is the opposite. – irony lies in the contrast between effort (connect digitally) and outcome (isolation).
X. Assess potential challenges/benefits of relying on survey outcomes for health interventions (para 5).
Benefits: Targeted strategies based on actual data; Challenges: Surveys may have biases, self-reporting errors, and may not capture deep behavioural nuances.

📄 Passage 2: Snacking Preferences Survey

I. Complete suitably: In introduction, linking hasty lifestyle with snack choice highlights...
...that modern hurried lifestyles often lead to unhealthy snacking, making it crucial to study preferences for better health outcomes.
II. "To examine snacking preferences across age groups and assess health implications" is classified as:
A. Primary purpose (the main goal of the study).
III. Give two points why fresh fruits were likely an option in the survey.
1. Fruits are a common, healthy snack option. 2. To compare health-conscious choices (like fruits) against packaged snacks, revealing diet patterns.
IV. Classify as 'influence' or 'motivation':
Sentence 1: Peer pressure leads teenagers to prefer packaged snacks → influence (external).
Sentence 2: Drive to maintain health makes middle-aged choose fruits → motivation (internal).
V. Identify Seema, Mohan, Arindam's demographics:
B. Seema – teenager; Mohan – middle-age; Arindam – child. (Seema's chips/cool drinks = teen; Mohan oranges/nuts = middle-age health conscious; Arindam mix = child).
VI. Why is preference for seasonal fruits highest among elderly?
Elderly have highest health concerns, digestive ease, and traditional belief in natural foods; they prioritize nutrition over convenience.
VII. How can targeted interventions improve health outcomes?
By addressing specific needs: for teens – tackle peer influence; for children – educate parents; for elderly – promote fruits. Tailored campaigns increase effectiveness.
VIII. Ultimate goal for stakeholders based on insights?
B. Building healthier communities.

A. Keeping Quiet (Extract based)
I. Common outcome of wars: Total destruction with 'no survivors' (victory with no survivors).
II. 'Walking with brothers in shade' represents: C. A moment of unity and peaceful reflection.
III. 'Clean clothes' symbolises: outward purity or pretence of innocence while they cause destruction.
IV. Speaker (condemns) the destructive nature of war.
V. Assertion & Reason: B. Both true but reason not correct explanation. (Doing nothing = introspection, not just stopping action).
VI. Message applied: We must pause and reflect; wars lead to mutual loss; choosing stillness can prevent annihilation.
B. Roadside Stand (Extract based)
I. Phrase suggesting imposed help: "enforcing benefits".
II. 'Swarm' does NOT represent: A. Coordinated help. (It represents overwhelming force, discomfort).
III. 'Ancient way' refers to: natural, traditional sleep (undisturbed by modern interventions).
IV. Illustrates: irony (greedy good-doers are contradictory).
V. Assertion & Reason: C. Assertion is true, but reason is false. (It instills lethargy, not ambition).
VI. Caution to rural poor: Beware of false benefactors who soothe you with promises but exploit your dependence.

📝 10. Five out of Six Short Questions (40-50 words each)

I. Hope/Resilience in Lost Spring?
Saheb's determination to find a rupee in garbage shows resilience despite abject poverty. His eyes light up with hope, reflecting theme of unquenchable human spirit even in stolen childhood.
II. Visual imagery by Kamala Das for passage of time?
In 'My Mother at Sixty-Six', she uses images like 'late winter's moon', 'ashen face' to depict ageing. The 'young trees' and 'merry children' contrast with mother's decay, showing time's fleeting nature.
III. Church clock vs Prussian trumpets in Last Lesson?
Church clock represents peaceful, orderly French life; Prussian trumpets symbolize harsh German military force. Their contrast marks the violent takeover of Alsace and loss of linguistic freedom.
IV. Author's style in 'The Interview' affects understanding?
The writer uses both perspectives (Ekland's praise and Umberto Eco's modesty) to present interview as a complex genre—both 'an art' and 'a threat'—making readers reflect on its dual nature.
V. Rattrap symbolises dual aspects of human nature?
Rattrap signifies the world's temptations that trap people (material greed). Yet, it also shows kindness (Edla's) can redeem, revealing both the baser and nobler sides of human nature.
VI. Factors instrumental in peasants' victory in Indigo?
Gandhi's leadership, his resolve, mass support, and legal astuteness (appointing Charles Andrews) united the peasants. The authorities capitulated due to moral pressure and fear of mass uprising.

📝 11. Any two out of three (40-50 words each)

I. Significance of toy tiger in 'Tiger King' climax?
The wooden toy tiger, though harmless, ironically causes the king's death (by a splinter). It fulfills the prophecy that the king would be killed by a tiger, highlighting the absurdity of fate.
II. Literal and metaphorical journeys in 'The Third Level'?
Literal journey is Charley's supposed train ride to Galesburg. Metaphorically, it's an escape from modern anxiety into a peaceful past; also his psychological journey to find security.
III. Pessimism and optimism in 'On the Face of It'?
Mr. Lamb's optimistic outlook ("life is what you make") contrasts Derry's pessimism (burnt face). Their interaction brings hope; yet Lamb's accidental death leaves a tinge of sadness—blend of both.

📝 12. Long answer (120-150 words) – Any one

A. Inequality in 'A Roadside Stand' and 'Aunt Jennifer's Tigers'
Both poems portray exploitation. In 'Roadside Stand', the rural poor are ignored by city dwellers; their poverty is perpetuated by an uncaring system. Similarly, Aunt Jennifer's oppressed married life symbolizes gender inequality; her heavy ordeals reflect male dominance. The impact: both bearers suffer silently—the villagers wait endlessly for aid that never comes; Aunt embroiders tigers as a fantasy of freedom. The tigers prance proudly, contrasting her subjugation. Inequality robs them of dignity and joy, but art offers fleeting escape. Frost's poem exposes economic disparity, while Rich's highlights patriarchy. Both demand empathy and structural change.

📝 13. Long answer (120-150 words) – Any one

A. Influence of traditions (religion, family, patriotism) in 'The Enemy'
Pearl S. Buck uses tradition to drive conflict. Dr. Sadao's patriotism (Japanese) clashes with his professional ethics as a doctor. His father's traditional values of honour and duty influence Sadao's initial dilemma. Family bonds (Hana's support) motivate them to save the American POW despite risk. The servant's superstition (belief that救治白人 brings bad luck) adds tension. Ultimately, Sadao transcends narrow patriotism through universal humanism, yet tradition of loyalty to nation remains. The setting—wartime Japan—and the motivation to uphold family name create narrative depth. The pacing slows at moral dilemmas, emphasizing inner conflict between tradition and humanity.
