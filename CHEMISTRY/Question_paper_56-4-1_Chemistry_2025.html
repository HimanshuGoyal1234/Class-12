A Multiple choice questions (1‑16) · 1 mark each
Q1
In an electrochemical cell: 2Cu⁺(aq) + Zn(s) → 2Cu(s) + Zn²⁺(aq), E° = 1.28 V. As reaction progresses, what happens to overall voltage?
(A) constant (B) decrease as [Zn²⁺]↑ (C) increase as [Cu⁺]↑ (D) increase as [Zn²⁺]↑
✅ Answer (B)
From Nernst equation: E = E° – (0.059/n) log([Zn²⁺]/[Cu⁺]²). As [Zn²⁺] increases, log term increases → E decreases.
Q2
Fe³⁺, Sc³⁺, Cr³⁺, Co³⁺ — which is colourless in aqueous solution? (Atomic: Fe=26, Sc=21, Cr=24, Co=27)
✅ Answer (A) Sc³⁺
Sc³⁺ has [Ar] configuration (no d‑electron), no d‑d transition. Others have unpaired d‑electrons → coloured.
Q3
Hoffmann Bromamide degradation is given by: (A) ArNO₂ (B) ArNH₂ (C) ArCONH₂ (D) ArCH₂NH₂
✅ Answer (C) ArCONH₂
Aromatic amide with Br₂/NaOH yields primary amine with one less carbon.
Q4
In the Haworth structure of carbohydrate (numbers shown), anomeric carbon is numbered: (A) 1 (B) 2 (C) 3 (D) 5
✅ Answer (A) 1
Anomeric carbon (C‑1 in aldoses, C‑2 in ketoses) is the carbonyl carbon that becomes chiral during cyclisation.
Q5
Henry's constant K_H is: (A) greater for higher solubility (B) greater for lower solubility (C) constant for all gases (D) not related
✅ Answer (B)
K_H is inversely proportional to solubility (p = K_H·x). Low solubility → high K_H.
Q6
Incorrect statement: (A) La is transition element (B) Zr & Hf have similar radii due to lanthanoid contraction (C) ionic radius La³⁺ → Lu³⁺ decreases (D) Lanthanoids are radioactive
✅ Answer (D)
Lanthanoids (except promethium) are not radioactive; actinoids are often radioactive.
Q7
Chiral molecule among: 2‑bromobutane, 1‑bromobutane, 2‑bromopropane, 1‑bromopropane.
✅ Answer (A) 2‑Bromobutane
2‑bromobutane (CH₃–CHBr–CH₂–CH₃) has a chiral carbon (four different substituents).
Q8
In the reaction sequence (image), structure of Y would be ?
✅ Answer (based on typical mechanism)
Without visual, likely Y is an alcohol or alkene from hydration/dehydrohalogenation. (Common: alkyl halide → alkene → alcohol etc.)
Q9
Oxidation of I⁻ by MnO₄⁻ in alkaline medium gives: (A) IO₄⁻ (B) I₂ (C) IO⁻ (D) IO₃⁻
✅ Answer (D) IO₃⁻
In alkaline medium MnO₄⁻ oxidises I⁻ to IO₃⁻ (iodate).
Q10
DDT is a: (A) greenhouse gas (B) fertilizer (C) biodegradable insecticide (D) non‑biodegradable insecticide
✅ Answer (D)
DDT is persistent in environment, non‑biodegradable.
Q11
Charge required to reduce 1 mole MnO₄⁻ to Mn²⁺: (A) 1F (B) 5F (C) 4F (D) 6F
✅ Answer (B) 5F
Mn⁺⁷ → Mn⁺² gains 5 electrons → 5 F per mole.
Q12
First step in dehydration of alcohol with conc. H₂SO₄: (A) carbocation formation (B) ester formation (C) protonation of alcohol (D) elimination of water
✅ Answer (C) protonation of alcohol
OH group protonated to make better leaving group (H₂O).
Q13
Assertion: Electrolysis of aq NaCl gives H₂ at cathode and Cl₂ at anode. Reason: Chlorine has higher oxidation potential than H₂O.
✅ Answer (C) Assertion true, Reason false
A is true (H₂, Cl₂ produced). R is false: Cl⁻ has lower oxidation potential than H₂O (H₂O oxidises at higher potential). Actually Cl⁻ oxidises easier.
Q14
Assertion: Cuprous salts are diamagnetic. Reason: Cuprous ion has fully filled 3d orbitals.
✅ Answer (A) Both true and R explains A
Cu⁺ (3d¹⁰) no unpaired electron → diamagnetic.
Q15
Assertion: n‑Butyl chloride has higher boiling point than n‑butyl bromide. Reason: C–Cl bond more polar than C–Br.
✅ Answer (D) Assertion false, Reason true
Bp: n‑butyl bromide (higher molar mass, stronger dispersion) > chloride. Polarity: C–Cl > C–Br, but mass dominates. A false, R true.
Q16
Assertion: Acetanilide is less basic than aniline. Reason: Acetylation reduces electron density on nitrogen.
✅ Answer (A) Both true and R explains
Amide resonance withdraws lone pair → less basic.
B Very short answer (2 marks each)
Q17(a)
Reactant A decomposition data: time(h) 0,1,2,3 ; [A](M) 0.40,0.20,0.10,0.05. Predict order and rate law.
✅ Answer
Concentration halves every hour → first order. Rate = k[A], k = 0.693 h⁻¹ (from t½ =1h).
Q17(b) OR
H₂ + I₂ reaction rate = k[H₂][I₂]. If 1 mol H₂ added at constant T, change in rate and rate constant?
✅ Answer
Rate increases (∵ [H₂]↑). Rate constant k remains same (T constant).
Q18
PtCl₄·2KCl does not give AgCl precipitate with AgNO₃. Write structural formula and IUPAC name.
✅ Answer
It is K₂[PtCl₆] (potassium hexachloroplatinate(IV)). No free Cl⁻ ions.
Q19
Define fuel cell. Two advantages over ordinary cell.
✅ Answer
Fuel cell: galvanic cell that converts chemical energy of fuel (e.g., H₂) to electricity continuously. Advantages: continuous supply, no recharging; less pollution; high efficiency.
Q20
Write structures of main products (reaction images).
✅ Answer
(Based on common patterns: e.g. alcohol oxidation, haloform reaction etc.) likely products: benzaldehyde → benzoic acid, or similar.
Q21
Essential amino acids? Why amino acids amphoteric?
✅ Answer
Essential amino acids: not synthesised by body, must be obtained from diet. Amphoteric due to both –NH₂ (basic) and –COOH (acidic) groups; form zwitterions.
C Short answer (3 marks each)
Q22(a)
Explain: (i) Allyl chloride hydrolysed faster than n‑propyl chloride. (ii) Alkyl halide + AgCN → isocyanide. (iii) Methyl chloride reacts faster with OH⁻ than t‑butyl chloride in SN2.
✅ Answer
(i) Allyl carbocation resonance stabilised. (ii) AgCN is covalent, CN⁻ attacks through N (ambident) → isocyanide. (iii) Methyl chloride less steric hindrance for SN2; t‑butyl chloride SN1 preferred.
Q23
Calculate cell voltage for Al/Al³⁺(0.001M) || Ni²⁺(0.1M)/Ni. E° Ni²⁺/Ni = –0.25V, Al³⁺/Al = –1.66V.
✅ Answer
E°cell = E°cathode – E°anode = –0.25 – (–1.66) = 1.41 V. Using Nernst: E = 1.41 – (0.059/6) log([Al³⁺]²/[Ni²⁺]³) = 1.41 – 0.00983 log(10⁻⁶/10⁻³) = 1.41 – 0.00983 log(10⁻³) = 1.41 – 0.00983×(–3) = 1.41 + 0.0295 = 1.4395 V.
Q24
Explain: (a) Mn³⁺ oxidising agent, Cr²⁺ reducing agent despite same d⁴. (b) Actinoid contraction greater than lanthanoid. (c) Transition metals form many interstitial compounds.
✅ Answer
(a) Mn³⁺ (d⁴) tends to gain electron to become half‑filled d⁵ (stable); Cr²⁺ (d⁴) loses e⁻ to become d³ (half‑filled t₂g). (b) Poorer shielding by 5f electrons → greater contraction. (c) Small atoms (H,B,C,N) fit in lattice voids; modify properties.
Q25
Aqueous NaOH osmotic pressure molar mass = 25 g/mol at 27°C. Calculate % dissociation. (Atomic Na=23,O=16,H=1)
✅ Answer
Theoretical M = 40 g/mol. Observed i = 40/25 = 1.6. For NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻, i = 1+α → α = 0.6 → 60% dissociated.
Q26
Arrange: (a) C₂H₅NH₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH, C₆H₅NHCH₃, C₆H₅NH₂ decreasing pKb. (b) C₂H₅OH, C₂H₅NH₂, (CH₃)₂NH increasing boiling point. (c) Increasing water solubility: C₆H₅NH₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH, C₂H₅NH₂.
✅ Answer
(a) pKb decreases with base strength. Order: C₆H₅NH₂ > C₆H₅NHCH₃ > C₂H₅NH₂ > (C₂H₅)₂NH. (b) Bp: (CH₃)₂NH < C₂H₅NH₂ < C₂H₅OH (H‑bonding). (c) Solubility: C₆H₅NH₂ < (C₂H₅)₂NH < C₂H₅NH₂.
Q27
Aromatic C₈H₈O gives positive 2,4‑DNP, yellow precipitate with NaOI, no reaction with Tollen’s/Fehling. Drastic oxidation gives acid C. Identify A,B,C.
✅ Answer
A = acetophenone (C₆H₅COCH₃). B = CHI₃ (iodoform). C = benzoic acid (C₆H₅COOH). IUPAC: A = 1‑phenylethanone, B = triiodomethane, C = benzoic acid.
Q28
Can sodium ethoxide + t‑butyl chloride give t‑butyl ethyl ether? Suggest appropriate starting material. IUPAC name of ether.
✅ Answer
No, elimination dominates (t‑butyl halide + strong base → alkene). Use t‑butyl alcohol + ethanol in acidic dehydration (Williamson fails for 3° halide). IUPAC: 2‑ethoxy‑2‑methylpropane.
D Case-based questions (4 marks each)
Q29
(a) Example miscible liquids showing negative deviation from Raoult’s law? Reason? (b)(i) State Raoult’s law for volatile components. OR (ii) “Raoult’s law is special case of Henry’s law” comment. (c) Two characteristics of ideal solution.
✅ Answer
(a) Chloroform‑acetone: H‑bonding → lower vapour pressure. (b)(i) For volatile components p₁ = x₁p₁°, p₂ = x₂p₂°. (ii) Henry’s law for gas in liquid; Raoult’s for solvent when mole fraction →1, both p ∝ x. (c) ΔmixH=0, ΔmixV=0, obeys Raoult’s law.
Q30
(a) Products when DNA hydrolysed? Structural difference DNA vs RNA? (b) Differentiate nucleotide and nucleoside. (c)(i) Two functions of nucleic acid. OR (ii) Linkage joining two nucleotides? Base present only in RNA?
✅ Answer
(a) DNA → phosphate + deoxyribose + adenine/guanine/cytosine/thymine. DNA: deoxyribose (no 2’‑OH), thymine; RNA: ribose, uracil. (b) Nucleoside = base + sugar; nucleotide = base + sugar + phosphate. (c)(i) Genetic information storage, protein synthesis. (ii) Phosphodiester bond; uracil.

Q32(a)
(i) Reasons: [Ni(CO)₄] diamagnetic, [NiCl₄]²⁻ paramagnetic; CO stronger complexing agent than NH₃; trans‑[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ optically inactive. (ii) Unpaired electrons in Fe³⁺ (d⁵) octahedral: strong field ligand vs weak field.

(ii) Fe³⁺ weak field: high spin d⁵ (5 unpaired). Strong field: low spin (t₂g⁵, one unpaired? Actually d⁵ low spin t₂g⁵ eg⁰ → 1 unpaired? careful: strong field → t₂g⁵ (all five in t₂g, 1 unpaired). Actually t₂g⁵ means one unpaired electron.
Q33(a)
(i) First order N₂O₅ decomposition: initial conc 1.2×10⁻², after 60 min = 0.2×10⁻² mol/L. Calculate rate constant (log6=0.778). (ii) Why order not from balanced eqn? Bimolecular reaction may become first order?
✅ Answer
(i) k = (2.303/60) log(1.2/0.2) = (2.303/60)×log6 = (2.303/60)×0.778 ≈ 0.0299 min⁻¹. (ii) Order determined experimentally; molecularity is theoretical. Bimolecular reaction with one reactant in large excess (pseudo‑first order, e.g. hydrolysis).
