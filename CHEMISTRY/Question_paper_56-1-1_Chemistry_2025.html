SECTION – A (Multiple Choice & Assertion-Reason)
Q1
In case of association, abnormal molar mass of solute will:
(A) increase (B) decrease (C) remain same (D) first increase then decrease
✅ Answer: (A) increase.
Association leads to higher apparent molar mass due to formation of larger aggregates, causing abnormal colligative effects (molar mass > expected).
Q2
Standard electrode potential for Sn⁴⁺/Sn²⁺ = +0.15 V and Cr³⁺/Cr = –0.74 V. The cell potential in standard state is:
(A) +1.19 V (B) +0.89 V (C) +0.18 V (D) +1.83 V
✅ Answer: (B) +0.89 V.
Sn⁴⁺/Sn²⁺ is cathode (higher reduction potential), Cr³⁺/Cr is anode. E°cell = E°cathode – E°anode = 0.15 – (–0.74) = 0.89 V.
Q3
Spin only magnetic moment of Cr³⁺ ion (Atomic no. 24):
(A) 2.87 B.M. (B) 3.87 B.M. (C) 3.47 B.M. (D) 3.57 B.M.
✅ Answer: (B) 3.87 B.M.
Cr³⁺ has 3 unpaired electrons (3d³). μ = √[n(n+2)] = √15 ≈ 3.87 B.M.
Q4
Acidified KMnO₄ oxidises sulphite to:
(A) S₂O₃²⁻ (B) S₂O₈²⁻ (C) SO₂(g) (D) SO₄²⁻
✅ Answer: (D) SO₄²⁻.
KMnO₄ oxidises sulphite (SO₃²⁻) to sulphate (SO₄²⁻) in acidic medium.
Q5
Correct IUPAC name of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]²⁺ is:
(A) Diamminedichlorodiplatinum (II) (B) Diamminedichlorodiplatinum (IV) (C) ... (D) ...
✅ Answer: (A) Diamminedichlorodiplatinum (II).
Two Pt centres each with +2 oxidation state; bridging chloro and ammine ligands. The complex is binuclear.
Q6
Arrange compounds in increasing boiling point (image had compounds: likely alcohols/aldehydes). As per typical pattern:
(A) ii < i < iii (B) i < ii < iii (C) iii < i < ii (D) iii < ii < i
✅ Answer: (D) iii < ii < i.
Based on H-bonding and molar mass: propanal < propan-2-ol < propan-1-ol (assuming i,ii,iii accordingly).
Q7
Alkyl halides undergoing nucleophilic bimolecular substitution (SN2) involve:
(A) retention (B) racemic mixture (C) inversion (D) carbocation
✅ Answer: (C) inversion of configuration.
SN2 proceeds through backside attack, leading to Walden inversion.
Q8
Correct order of acid strength:
(A) C₆H₅OH > H₂O > ROH (B) C₆H₅OH > ROH > H₂O (C) ROH > C₆H₅OH > H₂O (D) H₂O > C₆H₅OH > ROH
✅ Answer: (A) C₆H₅OH > H₂O > ROH.
Phenol is more acidic than water and alcohols due to resonance stabilisation of phenoxide.
Q9
Propyl magnesium bromide + CO₂ followed by acid hydrolysis gives:
(A) C₃H₇COOH (B) C₂H₅COOH (C) CH₃COOH (D) C₃H₇OH
✅ Answer: (A) C₃H₇COOH.
Grignard reagent with CO₂ yields carboxylic acid after hydrolysis (butanoic acid).
Q10
Best reagent for converting propanamide to propanamine:
(A) excess H₂ (B) Br₂ in aq NaOH (C) I₂/red P (D) LiAlH₄ in ether
✅ Answer: (B) Br₂ in aq NaOH (Hofmann bromamide degradation).
This yields amine with one less carbon.
Q11
Which statement is NOT true about glucose?
(A) It is an aldohexose. (B) On heating with HI forms n-hexane. (C) Exists in furanose form. (D) Does not give Schiff’s test.
✅ Answer: (C) It exists in furanose form.
Glucose predominantly exists in pyranose form (6-membered ring), not furanose.
Q12
Unripe mango in concentrated salt solution shrivels because:
(A) gains water by osmosis (B) loses water by reverse osmosis (C) gains water reverse osmosis (D) loses water by osmosis
✅ Answer: (D) it loses water due to osmosis.
Hypertonic salt solution causes exosmosis (water moves out of mango cells).
Q13
Assertion (A): [Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₂ and [Fe(H₂O)₆]Cl₂ are homoleptic complexes.
Reason (R): All ligands attached are same.
✅ Answer: (A) Both true, R correct explanation.
Homoleptic = all ligands identical; both complexes have only H₂O ligands.
Q14
A: Boiling points RI > RBr > RCl > RF. R: Boiling points of alkyl halides > hydrocarbon of comparable mass.
✅ Answer: (B) Both true, R not correct explanation.
Order due to increasing polarizability, R states a general fact but not explaining the exact order among halides.
Q15
A: Boiling point ethanol higher than methoxymethane. R: Intramolecular H-bonding in ethanol.
✅ Answer: (C) A is true, R false.
Ethanol has intermolecular H-bonding, not intramolecular; methoxymethane lacks H-bonding.
Q16
A: All natural α‑amino acids except glycine are optically active. R: Most have L-configuration.
✅ Answer: (B) Both true, R not correct explanation.
Optical activity due to chiral centre; L-configuration doesn’t guarantee activity, glycine is achiral.

Q17 (A)

Q19
Benzaldehyde more/less reactive in nucleophilic addition than propanal? Justify.
✅ Answer: Less reactive. In benzaldehyde, the carbonyl carbon is less electrophilic due to resonance with phenyl ring (electron donation by conjugation) and steric hindrance. Propanal is more reactive.

SECTION – C (3 marks each)
Q22
Glucose soln boiling point 100.20°C. Given Kf = 1.86, Kb = 0.512 K kg mol⁻¹. Find freezing point.
✅ Answer: ΔTb = 0.20°C = Kb·m ⇒ m = 0.20/0.512 ≈ 0.3906 mol/kg. ΔTf = Kf·m = 1.86 × 0.3906 ≈ 0.727°C. Freezing point = 0 – 0.727 = –0.727°C.
Q23
(a) State Kohlrausch law & Faraday's first law. (b) E°(X²⁺/X) = –2.36 V, E°(Y²⁺/Y) = –0.14 V, E°(Fe²⁺/Fe) = –0.44 V. Better coating for iron to prevent corrosion?
✅ Answer: (a) Kohlrausch: limiting molar conductivity = sum of individual ions. Faraday I: mass deposited ∝ charge passed.
(b) X (with more negative E°) is better sacrificial anode (more easily oxidised than Fe). Y is less reducing than Fe, so cannot protect Fe.
Q24
First order reaction: 50% in 20 min at 300 K, 50% in 5 min at 350 K. Calculate activation energy. (log 4 = 0.602, R = 8.314)
✅ Answer: t₁/₂ ∝ 1/k. k₂/k₁ = (t₁/₂)₁/(t₁/₂)₂ = 20/5 = 4. Using Arrhenius: log(k₂/k₁) = (Ea/2.303R)(1/T₁ – 1/T₂).
0.602 = (Ea/2.303×8.314) (1/300 – 1/350) = (Ea/19.14) (0.0004762) → Ea = 0.602×19.14 / 0.0004762 ≈ 24200 J/mol = 24.2 kJ/mol.
Q25
3d series Sc to Zn. (a) Why Cu has exceptionally positive E°(M²⁺/M)? (b) strong reducing agent in +2 state? (c) Zn²⁺ salts colourless?
✅ Answer: (a) High energy of atomisation & low hydration enthalpy due to d¹⁰ configuration. (b) Cr²⁺ (d⁴) easily oxidised to d³, strong reducing agent. (c) Zn²⁺ has 3d¹⁰ full filled, no d-d transition, hence colourless.

Draw major monohalo product for each (image based: assumed electrophilic substitution).

Q26 (B)
(a) Chlorobenzene to biphenyl (b) Propene to 1‑iodopropane (c) 2‑bromobutane to but‑2‑ene.

Q27
(a) Increasing boiling point: (CH₃)₂NH, CH₃CH₂NH₂, CH₃CH₂OH. (b) Explain: (i) Aromatic amines not by Gabriel synthesis (ii) Amides less basic than amines.
✅ Answer: (a) (CH₃)₂NH (secondary amine, some H‑bond) < CH₃CH₂NH₂ (primary amine, stronger H‑bond) < CH₃CH₂OH (strong O–H⋯ H‑bond).
(b) (i) Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with phthalimide ion. (ii) In amides, lone pair on N delocalised with carbonyl, less available for protonation.
Q28
(a) Difference between native & denatured protein. (b) Disaccharide among glucose, lactose, amylose, fructose? (c) Vitamin responsible for blood coagulation?
✅ Answer: (a) Native has specific 3D structure (active); denatured loses tertiary structure (unfolded), biological activity lost. (b) Lactose (galactose+glucose) is disaccharide. (c) Vitamin K (coagulation).

Q29
(a)(i) Rate determining step? (ii) Complex reaction? (b) Effect of temperature on rate constant? (OR: why molecularity only for elementary?) (c) X → Y, second order, [X]×3 → rate change?
✅ Answer: (a)(i) Slowest step in multistep reaction. (ii) Reaction that occurs in two or more steps.

Q30
Phenol: (a)(i) Br₂/CS₂ (ii) conc. HNO₃ (b) Why phenol not protonated easily? (c) Stronger acid: phenol or cresol? OR IUPAC name of Reimer‑Tiemann product.
✅ Answer: (a)(i) p‑bromophenol (mono) in CS₂. (ii) Picric acid (2,4,6‑trinitrophenol).
(b) After protonation, resonance of oxygen lone pair with ring lost, so unfavourable.
(c) Phenol is stronger acid than cresol (cresol has electron‑donating CH₃ destabilising phenoxide). OR Reimer‑Tiemann: salicylaldehyde (2‑hydroxybenzaldehyde).

Q31 (A)
(a) Cell: Sn|Sn²⁺(0.004M) || H⁺(0.02M)|H₂(1 bar)|Pt. Write cell reaction & e.m.f at 298K (E°Sn²⁺/Sn = –0.14 V, E°H⁺/H₂ = 0). (b) (i) In aq NaCl, Cl₂ liberated at anode instead of O₂? (ii) Why conductivity of CH₃COOH decreases on dilution?
✅ Answer: (a) Reaction: Sn + 2H⁺ → Sn²⁺ + H₂. E°cell = 0 – (–0.14)= 0.14 V. Ecell = 0.14 – (0.0591/2) log([Sn²⁺]/[H⁺]²) = 0.14 – 0.02955 log(0.004 / (0.02)²) = 0.14 – 0.02955 log(0.004/0.0004) = 0.14 – 0.02955 log(10) = 0.14 – 0.02955 = 0.11045 V ≈ 0.11 V.
(b)(i) Overpotential of O₂ is high, Cl⁻ oxidation occurs more easily. (ii) For weak electrolyte, dilution increases dissociation but number of ions per unit volume decreases initially? Actually conductivity decreases due to less ions per cm³ (molar conductivity increases).
OR
31 (B) Lead storage battery reactions; calculate half‑cell potential for Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺: [Cr₂O₇²⁻]=0.01 M, [Cr³⁺]=0.01 M, [H⁺]=1×10⁻⁴ M, E°=1.33 V.
✅ Answer: (a) Anode: Pb + SO₄²⁻ → PbSO₄ + 2e⁻; Cathode: PbO₂ + SO₄²⁻ + 4H⁺ + 2e⁻ → PbSO₄ + 2H₂O; Overall: Pb + PbO₂ + 2H₂SO₄ → 2PbSO₄ + 2H₂O.
(b) E = 1.33 – (0.0591/6) log([Cr³⁺]²/([Cr₂O₇²⁻][H⁺]¹⁴)) = 1.33 – (0.00985) log( (0.01)² / (0.01 × (10⁻⁴)¹⁴) ) = 1.33 – 0.00985 log(10⁻² / 10⁻⁵⁶) = 1.33 – 0.00985 log(10⁵⁴) = 1.33 – 0.00985×54 = 1.33 – 0.5319 = 0.798 V.
Q32 (A)
