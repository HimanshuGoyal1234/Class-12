Q1
The charge required for the reduction of 1 mol of MnO₄⁻ to MnO₂ is

(A) 1 F

(B) 3 F

(C) 5 F

(D) 6 F

✅ Answer (B) 3 F
MnO₄⁻ → MnO₂ ; Mn changes from +7 to +4 (gain of 3 e⁻ per Mn). For 1 mol MnO₄⁻, charge = 3 F.
Q2
Which among the following is a false statement?

(A) Rate of zero order reaction is independent of initial concentration of reactant.

(B) Half-life of a zero order reaction is inversely proportional to the rate constant.

(C) Molecularity of a reaction may be zero.

(D) For a first order reaction, t₁/₂ = 0.693/k

✅ Answer (C) false
Molecularity is always a positive integer (1,2,3...). It cannot be zero. All others are correct.
Q3
The number of molecules that react with each other in an elementary reaction is a measure of the :

(A) activation energy

(B) stoichiometry

(C) molecularity

(D) order

✅ Answer (C) molecularity
For an elementary step, molecularity = number of reacting species.
Q4
The element having [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ electronic configuration is

(A) Cu

(B) Zn

(C) Cr

(D) Mn

✅ Answer (A) Cu
Cu (Z=29) : [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (anomalous). Zn = 3d¹⁰4s²; Cr = 3d⁵4s¹; Mn = 3d⁵4s².
Q5
The complex ions [Co(NH₃)₅(NO₂)]²⁺ and [Co(NH₃)₅(ONO)]²⁺ are called

(A) Ionization isomers

(B) Linkage isomers

(C) Co-ordination isomers

(D) Geometrical isomers

✅ Answer (B) Linkage isomers
NO₂⁻ can bind through N (nitro) or O (nitrito).
Q6
The diamagnetic species is : [Co=27, Fe=26, Ni=28]

(B) [NiCl₄]²⁻

Q8
Oxidation of secondary alcohol with chromic anhydride (CrO₃) gives:

(A) Aldehyde

Q9
Conversion of phenol to salicylic acid is accomplished by:

(A) Reimer‑Tiemann

(B) Friedel‑Crafts

(C) Kolbe reaction

(D) Coupling

✅ Answer (C) Kolbe reaction
Phenol + NaOH + CO₂ under pressure, then acidification → salicylic acid (Kolbe–Schmitt).
Q10
Examples of denaturation of protein:

(A) Coagulation of egg white

(B) Curdling of milk

(C) Clotting of blood

(D) Both (A) and (B)

✅ Answer (D) Both (A) and (B)
Denaturation = loss of native structure. Egg white coagulation and milk curdling are examples. Clotting involves more than denaturation.
Q11
Nucleotides are joined together by

(A) Glycosidic

(B) Peptide

In DNA/RNA, nucleotides connect via phosphodiester bonds between sugar and phosphate.
Q12

(A) Vitamin B1

Q13
Assertion (A): In a first order reaction, if the concentration of the reactant is doubled, its half-life is also doubled.
Reason (R): The half‑life of a reaction does not depend upon the initial concentration of the reactant in a first order reaction.
✅ Answer (D) A false, R true
For first order, t₁/₂ is independent of initial conc. So A is false, R true.
Q14
Assertion (A): Cu cannot liberate H₂ on reaction with dilute mineral acids.
Reason (R): Cu has positive electrode potential.
✅ Answer (A) Both true, R correct explanation
E°Cu²⁺/Cu = +0.34V (positive). Cu cannot reduce H⁺ (requires negative E°).
Q15
Assertion (A): Aromatic primary amines cannot be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis.
Reason (R): Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the anion formed by phthalimide.
✅ Answer (A) Both true, R correct explanation
Gabriel uses alkyl halides (SN2); aryl halides are unreactive towards nucleophile.
Q16
Assertion (A): Vitamin D cannot be stored in our body.
Reason (R): Vitamin D is fat soluble vitamin and is not excreted from the body in urine.
✅ Answer (D) A false, R true
Vitamin D is fat‑soluble (stored in liver, fatty tissues). A is false; R true.
✦ SECTION B (2 marks each)
Q17 (A)
Rate constant for zero order A → P = 0.0030 mol L⁻¹s⁻¹. Initial conc 0.10 M to 0.075 M. Find time.
✅ Answer
For zero order: [A]ₜ = [A]₀ – kt ⇒ t = ([A]₀ – [A]ₜ)/k = (0.10 – 0.075)/0.0030 = 0.025/0.0030 = 8.33 s.
Q17 (B) OR
Decomposition of NH₃ on Pt: zero order, k = 2.5×10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹. Rates of production of N₂ and H₂?
✅ Answer
2NH₃ → N₂ + 3H₂. Rate = –½ d[NH₃]/dt = k = 2.5×10⁻⁴. So d[N₂]/dt = k = 2.5×10⁻⁴, d[H₂]/dt = 3k = 7.5×10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹s⁻¹.
Q18
Define: (a) Pseudo first order reaction (b) Half-life period.
✅ Answer
(a) Reaction that appears to be first order due to high excess of one reactant (e.g., hydrolysis of ester).
(b) Time taken for initial concentration to reduce to half, t₁/₂ = 0.693/k for first order.
Q19
Explain: (a) Transition elements form coloured compounds. (b) Zn not regarded as transition element.
✅ Answer
(a) Due to d-d transitions of unpaired d-electrons. (b) Zn has full d¹⁰ configuration in both ground state and common ions; no partly filled d subshell.

✦ SECTION C (3 marks each)
Q22
At 25°C vapour pressure of water = 24 mm Hg. Find vapour pressure of 5% aqueous urea solution (molar mass urea 60).
✅ Answer
5% = 5 g urea in 100 g solution → water = 95 g. n_urea = 5/60 = 0.08333 mol; n_water = 95/18 = 5.2778 mol; mole fraction water = 5.2778/(5.2778+0.08333)=0.9845. p = p°× X_water = 24×0.9845 = 23.63 mm Hg.
Q23
0.05 M NaOH column area 0.8 cm², length 40 cm, resistance 5×10³ ohm. Find resistivity, conductivity, molar conductivity.
✅ Answer
R = ρ(l/A) → ρ = R·A/l = 5000×(0.8×10⁻⁴ m²)/(0.4 m) = 5000×2×10⁻⁴ = 1.0 Ωm. Conductivity κ = 1/ρ = 1.0 S m⁻¹. Molar cond. Λₘ = κ/c = 1.0 / 50 = 0.02 S m² mol⁻¹ = 200 S cm² mol⁻¹ (since 1 S m²/mol = 10⁴ S cm²/mol? careful: 1 S m⁻¹ = 0.01 S cm⁻¹; better in S cm² mol⁻¹: κ = 0.01 S cm⁻¹, c=0.05 mol/L = 5×10⁻⁵ mol cm⁻³, Λₘ = 0.01/(5e-5)=200 S cm² mol⁻¹).

Q25
VBT: hybridization & magnetic behaviour of [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ and [Ni(CO)₄].
✅ Answer
[Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺: Co³⁺ = 3d⁶, strong NH₃ → d²sp³, inner orbital, diamagnetic (all paired). [Ni(CO)₄]: Ni(0) = 3d⁸4s², CO strong field → sp³, tetrahedral, diamagnetic.
Q26
(a) Define enantiomers, racemic mixture. (b) Why chlorobenzene resistant to nucleophilic substitution?
✅ Answer
(a) Enantiomers: non‑superimposable mirror images. Racemic mixture: equimolar mixture of enantiomers, optically inactive. (b) Due to resonance, C–Cl bond has partial double bond character; also sp² carbon is more electronegative; and aryl cation unstable.
Q27 (A)
Explain with equations: (a) Wolff‑Kishner reduction (b) Etard reaction (c) Cannizzaro reaction.
✅ Answer
(a) Carbonyl → alkane via hydrazine, KOH. (b) Toluene → benzaldehyde using CrO₂Cl₂. (c) Aldehyde without α‑H → alcohol + carboxylic acid (e.g., 2HCHO + OH⁻ → CH₃OH + HCOO⁻).
Q28
Define: glycosidic linkage, invert sugar, oligosaccharides.
✅ Answer
Glycosidic linkage: ether bond between two monosaccharides. Invert sugar: equimolar mixture of glucose+fructose from sucrose hydrolysis (dextrorotatory to laevorotatory). Oligosaccharides: 2‑10 monosaccharide units.

Q30
[aniline derivatives basicity order]
(a) Increasing basic order (given structures) and reason. (b) Why pKb aniline > methylamine? (c)(i) Arrange (CH₃)N, (CH₃)₂NH, NH₃, CH₃NH₂ in increasing basicity in water OR (c)(ii) Why ammonolysis not good for pure amines?
✅ Answer
(a) Aniline < p‑toluidine < p‑anisidine (electron donating increases basicity). (b) Aniline: lone pair delocalised into benzene ring; methylamine: +I of CH₃ increases basicity → lower pKb. (c)(i) NH₃ < (CH₃)₃N < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH (order in water: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃). (c)(ii) Ammonolysis gives mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines and quaternary salt.

Q32 (A)
(a) ΔG° for Au(s)+Ca²⁺→Au³⁺+Ca(s) using E°(Au³⁺/Au)=+1.5V, E°(Ca²⁺/Ca)= –2.87V. Spontaneous? (b) Can tarnish (Ag₂S) be removed by Al pan in NaCl? E° Ag₂S/Ag = –0.71V, E° Al³⁺/Al = –1.66V.
✅ Answer
(a) E°cell = cathode – anode = (–2.87) – (+1.5) = –4.37 V (non‑spontaneous). ΔG° = –nFE°cell = –6×96500×(-4.37)= +2530 kJ (positive, not spontaneous). (b) E°(Ag₂S/Ag) = –0.71 (cathode) ; Al³⁺/Al = –1.66 (anode) → E°cell = –0.71 – (–1.66) = +0.95 V >0, spontaneous, tarnish can be removed (Al reduces Ag⁺ to Ag).
